# Résumé Best Practices (Consultant / Fractional Executive)

A reusable guideline for a senior technology executive's résumé used as a business-development and credibility asset for an independent consulting / fractional and interim CTO / technical due-diligence practice. Use it to generate the résumé. The résumé copy itself is a separate artifact (in this repo, `geoff-scott-resume.md`). Best practices researched 2026-07.

Reader audience of the résumé: CEOs and founders, PE/VC investors and sponsors, boards. The résumé's job is to confirm credibility once interest exists, not to cold-close business.

---

## 0. The core distinction

A job-seeker résumé markets potential; a consultant's résumé documents delivered outcomes and scope so a buyer can assess risk. Every line should read "here is a problem like yours that I solved," not "here is what I was responsible for."

The résumé is a supporting asset. The BD engine is positioning: LinkedIn, the personal site, referrals, content, a capability one-pager. The résumé shows up in RFPs, PE and procurement vetting, diligence-panel intros, and the occasional W2 conversation. Its job is to confirm, not to sell cold.

Credibility is built by candor, not spin. This audience does diligence for a living, so a résumé that reads all-glory-no-nuance triggers skepticism. Own outcomes precisely (led versus contributed), prefer defensible ranges over unverifiable precision, and make sure everything survives a backchannel check. A big, checkable claim (a revenue share, an exit multiple) that doesn't hold up is worst of all on the résumé of someone who sells diligence; verify it or cut it.

---

## 1. Length, format, file, naming

- **Length:** the executive standard is 2 pages, and up to 3 only for formal board or diligence vetting. Consultant guidance is tighter: "if it feels dense, it's too long." A recruiter spends roughly 26 seconds, so the first third of page one must carry the pitch.
- **Format:** hybrid. A positioning line, a credibility bar, and a summary up top, then a Selected Deals highlights block, then compressed reverse-chronological history. Single column, clean, minimal graphics.
- **File type:** PDF for the human-read version (it locks formatting, and modern ATS parse text PDFs fine). Keep a DOCX for any portal that demands it. Never a scanned or image PDF.
- **Naming:** `First-Last-Resume.pdf`, and targeted cuts such as `First-Last-FractionalCTO.pdf` or `First-Last-Diligence.pdf`. No spaces, no version numbers.

---

## 2. Header, positioning line, credibility bar

- **Contact:** name, city or region only (no street address), email, LinkedIn, and, important for a consultant, the practice website. A public copy may omit phone and email; the version sent to a buyer should include a way to reach you and a link to your other BD assets.
- **Positioning line** (replaces the objective): a pipe-delimited statement of level and specialization.
- **Credibility bar:** 3–5 quantified proof points near the top. This is the fastest way to establish authority in the 26-second window, and it mirrors how PE operating partners open.

---

## 3. Summary / profile

3–5 lines, one idea per line. Forward-looking value proposition anchored by proof: what you deliver to the buyer, backed by concrete outcomes. Accomplishment-focused summaries reportedly out-pull objective-style openers by a wide margin.

---

## 4. Selected Deals (the most important structural choice)

Lead with a Selected Engagements / Transaction Experience block, not a straight job list. This is how PE operating partners present: 3–5 representative deals, each a line of context (stage, sector, deal type) plus 1–3 outcome bullets (entry-to-exit deltas, EBITDA or margin, cost takeout, revenue, exit or fundraise).

Outcome-first bullet formula: result, then metric, then mechanism. "Cut cloud spend 34% ($2.1M/yr) by re-architecting to reserved capacity." Quantify with business impact (percent, dollars, scale, before-and-after), not deliverables. Show, don't tell: replace "strategic" or "innovative" with the evidence that implies them. Curate for relevance over volume; if it feels long, cut the softest entries, not the strongest.

---

## 5. Portfolio / fractional career mechanics

- **Overlapping roles are expected.** Umbrella them under the practice so overlap reads as capacity, not job-hopping.
- **Label every engagement by role type:** Interim CTO, Fractional CTO, Advisor, Diligence Lead, Board Advisor. Buyers price these differently and will challenge a light advisory role dressed as an operating claim.
- **Consolidate many short engagements** under one title line with client sub-entries.
- **Confidential / NDA clients:** most NDAs let you acknowledge a project existed and share masked impact. Use descriptors ("Series-C fintech, $40M ARR"), ranges and percentages, and a private named-reference list you share when a serious buyer asks. Check whether older NDAs have expired, since a now-nameable client is stronger.

---

## 6. Section order and placement

Recommended: Summary, then Selected Deals, then Experience (the practice first, then prior exec roles), then Board and Advisory, then Patents, then Publications and Speaking, then Education.

- **Skills:** fold into the summary and demonstrate in bullets; avoid long generic competency lists. A tight technical and domain keyword line is fine.
- **Patents:** dedicated section, a strong CTO differentiator.
- **Board and Publications:** own sections; high-credibility for this audience.
- **Education:** after experience; a senior track record outranks the degree.

---

## 7. Audience variants (keep one master, cut variants)

- **Investor / diligence variant** (PE/VC deal team, board): lead with deal-fluent proof, such as diligences led, enterprise value assessed, risk found or repriced, exits supported. Foreground independence, objectivity, and the ability to communicate technical risk to a non-technical board. Structure around Selected Transaction Experience.
- **Operating-CTO variant** (founder or CEO hiring interim or fractional): lead with building and scaling proof, such as orgs scaled, delivery velocity, re-architecture, reliability, cost, and team leadership.
- Keep the factual core identical across variants, since consistency defends against backchannel checks. Change only the positioning line, which proof leads, and the section emphasis.

---

## 8. ATS is mostly irrelevant here

For human-read BD, optimize for the 26-second scan, not a parser. The "75% never seen by humans" line is a debunked myth (it traces to a defunct 2013 vendor); in reality ATS mostly rank and sort, and a recruiter reviews the pool. Keywords affect ranking, not a hard gate. Place real search terms in context (summary and engagements); never stuff or use hidden or white text, which is flagged and rejected on sight. Keep a clean single-column DOCX for the rare portal.

---

## 9. Dated conventions to drop

Objective statements, "references available upon request," a full street address, photo, age, and DOB, every job since the start of the career in full (compress early roles), generic soft-skill adjectives, skill rating bars, ornate multi-column templates, and version-numbered file names.

---

## 10. AI tells and voice

Follow `docs/voice-and-style.md`. This audience is unusually good at detection, and major ATS vendors shipped AI-content classifiers in late 2025. Purge the "LLM default" vocabulary and patterns:

- **Cliché words:** *spearheaded, orchestrated, leveraged, synergy, foster, transformative, deep dive, seamlessly, cutting-edge, dynamic, empower, strategic visionary, unwavering commitment, thought leader, game-changer, passionate, results-oriented, "at the intersection of."*
- **Structural tells:** uniform bullet cadence; vague grandiosity with no named system, number, or obstacle; text walls; random over-bolding; over-use of em-dashes; and a polished résumé paired with a thin LinkedIn (keep the two aligned).
- **Sound human:** inject specificity (real system names, deal contexts, the actual hard problem); vary sentence rhythm; read it aloud and cut anything unnaturally formal.

---

## How to use this guide

Generate the résumé from these principles and keep the résumé copy in the artifact file (here, `geoff-scott-resume.md`), not in this document. Keep this guide generic and reusable.
